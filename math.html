  • List each angle type and identify common angle measurements.

Terms :

Click for Definitions

Use these terms as the foundation for geometry, area, and volume calculations.

Plane Geometry :
The study of flat, two-dimensional shapes such as rectangles, triangles, and circles.
Solid Geometry :
The study of three-dimensional shapes such as boxes, cylinders, cones, spheres, and prisms.
Angle :
The shape formed when two straight lines meet at a point. Angles are measured in degrees.
Vertex :
The point where two or more lines or curves come together.
Degree :
A unit used to measure angles. A full circle has 360 degrees.
Acute Angle :
An angle greater than 0° and less than 90°.
Right Angle :
An angle that measures exactly 90°. The lines are perpendicular.
Obtuse Angle :
An angle greater than 90° and less than 180°.
Straight Angle :
A 180° angle, which appears as a straight line.
Adjacent Angles :
Angles that have the same vertex and one side in common.
Opposite Angles :
Angles formed by two straight lines crossing. Opposite angles are equal.
Rectangle :
A four-sided shape with four 90° angles. Opposite sides are parallel and equal in length.
Square :
A special rectangle with four equal sides and four 90° angles. Square also means a number multiplied by itself.
Triangle :
A closed shape with three sides and three angles. The angles inside a triangle total 180°.
Right Triangle :
A triangle that includes one 90° angle.
Circle :
A closed curved line drawn at a constant distance around a central point. A circle measures 360°.
Perimeter :
The distance around the outside of a closed shape.
Circumference :
The distance around a circle.
Radius :
The distance from the center of a circle to the outside edge. The radius is half the diameter.
Diameter :
The distance across a circle through the center point.
Area :
The amount of space inside a flat, two-dimensional shape. Area is measured in square units.
Volume :
The amount of space inside a three-dimensional object. Volume is measured in cubic units.
Pi (π) :
A constant used with circles. For this class, use π = 3.14.

Calculating Area :

Area is the space inside a flat shape. Because area is two-dimensional, the answer uses square units like in2, ft2, m2, or cm2.

Rectangle Area Example

Calculate the area of a rectangle that is 15.6 meters × 22.3 meters.

22.3 m
Area = L × W
15.6 m high

Formula: Area = Length × Width

Work: 15.6 × 22.3 = 347.88

Answer: 347.88 m2

Tech Tip - Industrial Maintenance: Control cabinet layouts use area thinking. A technician needs enough panel space for drives, relays, terminal blocks, wire duct, and cooling clearance.

Triangle Area Example

A triangular bracket has a base of 12 inches and a height of 8 inches. Find the area.

Base = 12 in

Height = 8 in

Formula: Area = (Base × Height) ÷ 2

Work: (12 × 8) ÷ 2 = 96 ÷ 2 = 48

Answer: 48 in2

Circle Area and Circumference Example

A round inspection cover has a diameter of 10 inches. Find the circumference and area.

Diameter = 10 in   |   Radius = 5 in

Circumference Formula: Circumference = π × Diameter

Work: 3.14 × 10 = 31.4

Circumference Answer: 31.4 in

Area Formula: Area = π × Radius2

Work: 3.14 × 52 = 3.14 × 25 = 78.5

Area Answer: 78.5 in2

Tech Tip - Wind Technician: The swept area of a turbine rotor is a circle. Larger rotor diameter means a much larger swept area because circle area uses radius squared.

Calculating Volume :

Volume is the space inside a three-dimensional object. Because volume is three-dimensional, answers use cubic units like in3, ft3, yd3, cm3, or m3.

Rectangular Prism Volume Example

Calculate the volume of a shipping container that is 32 feet long × 11 feet wide × 2 feet deep.

Rectangular Prism

32 ft length × 11 ft width × 2 ft deep

Formula: Volume = Length × Width × Height

Work: 32 × 11 × 2 = 704

Answer: 704 ft3

Tech Tip - Industrial Maintenance: Volume calculations show up when estimating storage capacity, tank capacity, containment trays, and machine guarding enclosures.

Cylinder Volume Example

A cylindrical tank has a diameter of 22 feet and a height of 10 feet. Find the volume.

Diameter = 22 ft   |   Radius = 11 ft   |   Height = 10 ft

Formula: Volume = π × Radius2 × Height

Step 1: Diameter is 22 ft, so radius is 11 ft.

Step 2: 3.14 × 112 = 3.14 × 121 = 379.94 ft2

Step 3: 379.94 × 10 = 3,799.4

Answer: 3,799.4 ft3

Triangular Prism Volume Example

A triangular support has a base of 6 inches, height of 4 inches, and length of 20 inches. Find the volume.

 →

Triangle face: base = 6 in, height = 4 in   |   Prism length = 20 in

Formula: Volume = ((Base × Height) ÷ 2) × Length

Step 1: Triangle area = (6 × 4) ÷ 2 = 12 in2

Step 2: Volume = 12 × 20 = 240

Answer: 240 in3

Trapezoid Area Example

A tapered cover has Base1 = 8 inches, Base2 = 14 inches, and Height = 5 inches. Find the area.

Base1 = 8 in   |   Base2 = 14 in   |   Height = 5 in

Formula: Area = ((Base1 + Base2) × Height) ÷ 2

Work: ((8 + 14) × 5) ÷ 2 = (22 × 5) ÷ 2 = 110 ÷ 2 = 55

Answer: 55 in2

Right Triangle / Pythagorean Theorem Example

A robot cell needs a diagonal safety clearance. One side is 6 feet and the other side is 8 feet. Find the diagonal.

a = 6 ft   |   b = 8 ft   |   c = diagonal

Formula: c = √(a2 + b2)

Work: c = √(62 + 82) = √(36 + 64) = √100 = 10

Answer: 10 ft

Tech Tip - Robotics: Right triangles help estimate diagonal travel, reach, offsets, and clearances inside a robot work cell.

Problem 1: An angle measures 105°. What type of angle is it?

Reveal Answer & Steps
Answer: Obtuse angle.
An obtuse angle is greater than 90° and less than 180°. Since 105° is between those values, it is obtuse.

Problem 2: What is the distance around the outside of a closed shape called?

Reveal Answer & Steps
Answer: Perimeter.
Circumference is specifically the distance around a circle. Perimeter is the general term for the distance around a closed shape.
